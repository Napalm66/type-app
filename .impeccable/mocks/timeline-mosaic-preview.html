Era mosaic — figure/ground rectangle subdivision

Technique: recursive asymmetric splitting. Each band's area is cut in two at an uneven ratio (never 50/50 — that reads as a grid); one side is kept as a single larger "hero" tile, the other is cut again, and again, cascading into progressively smaller supporting tiles. Because each cut is an exact partition (not floating boxes), tiles always cover 100% of the area with zero gaps — separation between tiles is the 1px ink border alone, no shadow. Depth (how many times it keeps cutting) scales with the era's available width, so a wide era gets a rich cascade of 5-6 tiles and a narrow one stays to 1-2 — different counts per era, driven by space, not a fixed rule. Each era is seeded by its own name, so the same era always produces the same composition shape at a given width, rather than reshuffling randomly on every render — important since these bands resize live (equal-width at rest, fisheye-expanded on click).

Tonal variation on the placeholder tiles (alternating panel/paper/tinted fills) stands in for the actual variation real black-and-white photos would bring — once real photos are supplied, each tile becomes an <image> with this exact same size/position, cropped to fill via preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid slice".

At rest — every era band is the same width (equal-share layout). Notice the mosaic still varies era to era: the seed comes from the era's name, not its width, so Gothic and Romanticism (both roughly the same width here) still produce distinct compositions.

One era opened (Gothic, simulating a click) — same seed, recomputed for the new, much wider available width: more depth, richer cascade, same "shape language" as its resting-state version just scaled up with more room to subdivide, not a different random layout.
