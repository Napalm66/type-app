Timeline — lineage tree (concept, with era bands + watermark placeholders)

Same chronological x-axis as the current Gantt-style Timeline. Era bands (Romanesque through Digital & Variable Age — same names/dates/colors as the current Timeline) now run the full height, filled with equal-width vertical strips that fade to transparent toward the base — dissolving into the era's own color rather than ending on a hard edge — where real black-and-white era photos will drop in once supplied. Each era gets as many strips as its current width comfortably fits (one when narrow, several when a fisheye-opened era gets room), always equal width within that era. At rest, every era gets an equal share of the width, regardless of how long it actually lasted — a clean, uniform starting layout where every label fits on one line. Click an era band to open it: it expands to a fixed larger share of the chart and every other era compresses proportionally by its own real duration to make room — a true fisheye that reveals which eras actually ran long or short. Every classification box and connector shares that same distorted x-axis, so the lineage tree redistorts in sync. Click the open era again (or click a different one to switch directly to it) to close; closing back down to nothing open returns the scroll position to wherever it was before you opened the first one. This replaced an earlier hover-to-expand version — hovering merely to read a label while passing the cursor across the chart was triggering the same full redistortion by accident, which click fixes by requiring a deliberate action. Classification node names never wrap — each node's box extends past its natural date-driven width to fit its full name, pushing later nodes in the same lane over to make room.
